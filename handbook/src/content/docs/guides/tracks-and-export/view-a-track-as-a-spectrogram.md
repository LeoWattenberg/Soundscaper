## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

A waveform shows changes in amplitude, while a spectrogram shows how frequencies change over time. Switch one track to spectrogram view to inspect a tone, noise, or other frequency detail without changing its audio.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Track menu → Track Visualization → Spectrogram**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the recording whose frequencies you want to inspect. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Open the menu for the recording you want to inspect from the **Track menu** button in its header and choose **Track visualization → Spectrogram**. The track changes to a frequency display; its audio and clip timing are unchanged.
4. The project now shows 1 clip.
   *You should see:* The track shows a spectrogram while the clip remains on the same timeline.

## Tips

- Choose **Track visualization → Waveform** from the same track menu to return to the amplitude view.
- Spectrogram settings are available from the track menu when you need to adjust its frequency range or scale.
