## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

When a two-channel recorder puts one speaker on the left and another on the right, separating the channels lets you edit and balance each voice. Split stereo to centered mono makes two mono tracks, both panned to the center. It keeps their timeline positions and lengths, and leaves each channel as its own recording.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Track menu → Split stereo to center mono (Audacity 4)**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the stereo recording whose channels you want to separate. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Open the track's menu from the **Track menu** button in its header and choose **Track channels → Split stereo to centered mono**.
4. The project now shows 2 clips and 3 tracks.
   *You should see:* Separate Left and Right tracks hold the original channels, with both pan controls centered.

## Tips

- To keep the original stereo placement, [split into left/right mono tracks](/guides/tracks-and-export/split-stereo-into-mono-tracks/) instead.
- This creates two mono tracks. To combine their sound into one mono channel, [mix the tracks down](/guides/tracks-and-export/mix-tracks-into-one/) and choose Mono.
- **Edit → Undo** restores the stereo track.
