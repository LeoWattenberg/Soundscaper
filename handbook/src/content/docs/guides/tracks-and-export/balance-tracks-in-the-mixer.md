Track headers hold a volume slider each, but balancing a mix means seeing them together. The Mixer panel lays out one channel strip per track — name, volume fader, pan, mute and solo, and the track’s effects — beside a master strip, the way a mixing desk does. Audacity 3 had a Mixer Board for this; Audacity 4 has not brought it back.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **View → Mixer Board (Audacity 3; Audacity 4 has no mixer board)**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick the first track of the mix. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick the second track. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
4. Choose **View → Panels → Mixer**.
5. The project now shows the **Mixer** panel open.
   *You should see:* A strip for each track, with its Volume fader, Pan control and Mute/Solo buttons, and a Master strip at the end.
6. Drag a strip’s **Volume** fader to set that track’s level and turn **Pan** to place it left or right. These are the same controls as the track header, so either view can be used.
7. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The meters on each strip move with its track while the project plays.

## Tips

- **Add group bus** creates a bus that several tracks can feed, so a whole drum kit or a group of voices has one fader.
- The **Routing graph** button in the panel shows the same mixer as a diagram of what feeds what.
