Traffic, air conditioning, wind on the microphone and the desk being bumped all live below the voice, in frequencies a small speaker cannot even reproduce. A high-pass filter lets everything above a cutoff through and rolls off what is below it, cleaning up the low end without touching the speech.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → High-Pass Filter**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick the recording with the rumble. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select all**.
4. Choose **Effect → Legacy effects → Classic Filters**. In the **Classic Filters** dialog, choose **High-pass** for **Filter type** and set **Cutoff frequency** to `100`, then press **Apply to selection**. 100 Hz is safe for speech. Raise the order for a steeper roll-off.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The low rumble is gone and the voice sounds the same.

## Tips

- For music, start lower — around 40 Hz — so bass instruments are not thinned.
- The realtime **High-pass filter** in a track’s effect rack does the same job while you play, without rendering.
