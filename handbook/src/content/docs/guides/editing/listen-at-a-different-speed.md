Transcribing an interview, learning a solo or skimming a two-hour recording all go better at a speed other than real time. Play-at-speed changes only how fast you hear the project; nothing is rendered and the clips are exactly as they were when you stop. Audacity 3 kept a speed slider in its toolbar, which Audacity 4 has not brought back.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **The Play-at-Speed slider (Audacity 3; Audacity 4 has no play-at-speed)**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick the recording you want to listen to. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Press **Play options** beside the Play button, set **Playback speed** to `0.75×`, and press Escape to close the options. The Play button now reads **Play at speed**; press it to listen, then press **Pause play at speed**.
   *You should see:* The recording plays at three quarters of its speed.

## Tips

- **Preserve pitch** in the same options keeps a voice sounding natural while it is slowed; turn it off for a tape-style pitch drop.
- Set the speed back to `1×` and the button is an ordinary Play again. To change the recording itself, use [Change tempo](/guides/effects/change-tempo-without-changing-pitch/).
