Podcast and streaming platforms measure loudness in LUFS, which follows how loud a programme feels rather than how tall its peaks are. Loudness Normalization measures the whole selection and adjusts it to a target, so every episode you publish sits at the same level.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → Volume and Compression → Loudness Normalization**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick your recording. This guide uses `guide-quiet-take.wav`. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select all**.
4. Choose **Effect → Volume and compression → Loudness Normalization**. In the **Loudness Normalization** dialog, set **Target loudness** to `-16`, then press **Apply to selection**. −16 LUFS is the common target for stereo podcasts; −19 LUFS is often used for mono. Check your platform’s guidelines.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The take plays at a comfortable, consistent level.

## Tips

- Loudness Normalization can push peaks above full scale on very dynamic material. Follow it with a limiter or a peak Normalize to −1 dB if the waveform touches the top.
- Run it on the finished mix, after edits and other effects, so the measurement reflects what listeners will hear.
