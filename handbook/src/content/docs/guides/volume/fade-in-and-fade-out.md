A fade is the simplest way to avoid a click at the start of a clip or an abrupt stop at the end. Fade In ramps the selection up from silence over its whole length; Fade Out ramps it down. The length of the selection is the length of the fade.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → Fading → Fade In and Fade Out**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick your recording. This guide uses `guide-music-loop.wav`. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Drag in the ruler above the clip, from the start to the quarter point of the clip, to select that range. Select as much of the start as you want the fade to last.
4. Choose **Effect → Fading → Fade In**. The effect applies to the selection straight away.
5. Drag in the ruler above the clip, from the three-quarter point to the end of the clip, to select that range. Now select the same amount at the end.
6. Choose **Effect → Fading → Fade Out**. The effect applies to the selection straight away.
7. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The loop swells in and dies away instead of starting and stopping hard.

## Tips

- The fades are linear. For a quicker fade at the very end, select a shorter range.
- Fades are edits, so **Edit → Undo** reverses them like anything else.
