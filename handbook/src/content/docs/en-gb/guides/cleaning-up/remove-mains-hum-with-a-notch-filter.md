Mains hum sits at one frequency: 50 Hz in most of the world, 60 Hz in the Americas, sometimes with a harmonic at twice that. A notch filter removes a narrow band around one frequency, leaving everything else untouched, which is far gentler than a high-pass filter when the hum isn't low enough to roll off. The same tool can eliminate a monitor whistle or ringing resonance once you know its frequency.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → EQ and Filters → Notch Filter**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which may differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and select the recording with the hum. It will appear as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select all**.
4. Choose **Effect → EQ and filters → Notch filter**. In the **Notch filter** dialog, set **Notch frequency** to `50` and **Q** to `4`, then press **Apply to selection**. Use 60 if your mains frequency is 60 Hz. A Q of 4 creates a narrow notch; lower it if the hum varies, raise it if the cut is audible on the voice.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The hum is removed, and the voice remains unchanged.

## Tips

- Uncertain of the frequency? [Plot the spectrum](/guides/analysis/plot-a-spectrum/) of a quiet passage and read the peak.
- Hum often has a harmonic at double the frequency. Run the filter again at 100 or 120 Hz if some remains.
