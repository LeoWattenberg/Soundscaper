## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

A steady whistle or electronic tone can sit in a small frequency band for only part of a recording. Spectral Delete removes that band inside a time selection while leaving the surrounding frequencies in place.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → Spectral Tools → Delete selection (Audacity 4)**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the recording containing a brief tonal sound. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Open the menu for the track you want to inspect from the **Track options** button in its header and choose **Track visualization → Spectrogram**.
4. Drag in the ruler above the clip to select the time span where the tone is audible.
5. Choose **Effect → Spectral editing → Spectral box select**. Set **Minimum frequency (Hz)** to `200` and **Maximum frequency (Hz)** to `320`, then press **Spectral Delete**. This removes the loop’s 261.6 Hz tone only during the chosen time span.
6. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The tonal sound is reduced in that passage while the rest of the recording remains.

## Tips

- Use the spectrogram to locate the tone, then narrow the time and frequency bounds around it before deleting.
- Spectral Delete affects the selected time and frequency range; ordinary Delete removes the whole selected passage.
