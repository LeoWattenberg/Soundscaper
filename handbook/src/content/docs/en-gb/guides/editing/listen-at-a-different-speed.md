Transcribing an interview, learning a solo or skimming a two-hour recording all benefit from playing at a speed other than real time. Play-at-speed adjusts only the playback speed; nothing is rendered, and the clips remain unchanged when you stop. Audacity 3 had a dedicated **Play-at-Speed slider** (absent in Audacity 4).

:::note[Migrating from Audacity?]
This feature corresponds to Audacity's **Play-at-Speed slider (Audacity 3; Audacity 4 has no equivalent)**. Soundscaper uses its own naming conventions, which may differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Launch Soundscaper, creating a new, empty project.
2. Import your desired recording via **File → Import audio**. The file will appear as a clip on its own track.
3. Click the **Play options** button next to the Play button, set **Playback speed** to `0.75×`, then press Escape to close the options. The Play button will now display **Play at speed**. Click it to listen, then press **Pause play at speed**.
   *Observation:* The recording should play at three-quarters of its original speed.

## Tips

- **Preserve pitch** in the Play options ensures a natural-sounding voice when slowed down; disable it for a tape-style pitch drop.
- Reset the speed to `1×`, and the Play button reverts to its standard function. To permanently alter the recording's speed, use [Change tempo](/guides/effects/change-tempo-without-changing-pitch/).
