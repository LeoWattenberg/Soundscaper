## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

Insert makes room for copied audio instead of placing it over existing sound. Audio later on the destination track moves right by the length of the copied passage. Use it to add a repeated phrase or an extra sentence inside a recording.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity’s **Edit → Copy, then Edit → Paste**. The names below are Soundscaper’s own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the recording with the passage you want to copy. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Drag in the ruler above the clip to select the passage you want to insert elsewhere.
4. Choose **Edit → Copy**.
5. Click the waveform where the copy should begin inside the destination clip to put the cursor there. Clicking clears the time range and sets the insertion point.
6. Choose **Edit → Paste → Insert**.
7. The project now shows 3 clips.
   *You should see:* The inserted passage sits between two pieces of the original clip. The later piece has moved right, and the earlier piece has kept its position.

## Tips

- Insert moves later audio on the destination track. **Edit → Paste → Insert and preserve synchronisation** makes room across all tracks when their timing must stay linked.
- **Edit → Undo** removes the insertion and restores the later audio’s position.
