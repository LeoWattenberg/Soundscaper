## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

A shelf filter changes a broad range at one end of the spectrum and levels off beyond its transition. Choose a high shelf to brighten or soften the top end, or a low shelf to add or reduce weight.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → EQ and Filters → Shelf Filter**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the recording whose bass or treble you want to adjust. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → All**.
4. Choose **Effect → EQ and filters → Shelf filter**. In the **Shelf filter** dialog, choose **High shelf** for **Filter type**, set **Shelf frequency** to `6000` and set **Gain** to `2`, then press **Apply to selection**. A small high-shelf boost adds some air above 6 kHz while leaving the lower range mostly alone.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The top end is a little brighter while the lower frequencies remain steady.

## Tips

- Choose **Low shelf** and a lower frequency to adjust bass instead of treble.
- Large boosts can make a mix harsh or boomy. Try a small change and compare it with the original.
