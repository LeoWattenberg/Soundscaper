## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

A bitcrusher deliberately reduces the detail used to represent a sound, adding grain and digital edge. It works well on a synth, drum hit, or a short vocal phrase when clean audio feels too polished.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **No direct built-in equivalent in Audacity’s current effect list.**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the sound you want to roughen. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → All**.
4. Choose **Effect → Distortion and modulation → Bitcrusher**. In the **Bitcrusher** dialog, set **Bit depth** to `6` and set **Sample rate reduction** to `4`, then press **Apply to selection**. Fewer bits make the amplitude steps more audible; sample rate reduction lowers the detail in time.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The sound has a rough, stepped digital edge.

## Tips

- Change one control at a time so you can hear whether the roughness comes from amplitude steps or reduced sample detail.
- Lower **Mix** to blend the crushed sound with the original if the full effect is too harsh.
