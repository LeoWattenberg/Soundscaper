A phaser mixes a sound with a copy of itself passed through moving all-pass stages, creating notches that sweep up and down the spectrum in time with a slow oscillator. This effect is commonly heard on guitar, electric piano, or pads in numerous recordings. As Audacity 3 included a phaser effect, but Audacity 4 does not, this guide provides an alternative method to achieve the same result in Soundscaper.

:::note[From Audacity]
This is the equivalent of Audacity's **Effect → Phaser** (found in Audacity 3; Audacity 4 does not have a Phaser effect). The names used below are specific to Soundscaper and may differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Launch Soundscaper. A new, empty project will be ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Select **File → Import** and choose the sound you wish to process. It will be added as a clip on its own track.
3. Select **Select → Select all** to highlight the entire clip.
4. Navigate to **Effect → Distortion and modulation → Phaser**. In the **Phaser** dialog box, set the **Stages** to `4` and the **LFO frequency** to `0.6`, then click **Apply to selection**. More stages will create deeper notches; four stages is a classic setting often found on pedals. The LFO frequency determines the speed of the sweep - 0.6 Hz will result in a slow, musical swirl.
5. Press **Play** to listen to the effect, then **Stop**.
   *Observation:* You should notice the sound swirling slowly as the notches sweep through it.

## Tips

- **Feedback** can sharpen the notches, creating a more metallic, resonant sweep. Keep it close to zero for a subtler effect.
- Phaser can also be used as a realtime effect in a track’s rack, allowing you to hear the sweep as you adjust it: [Add a realtime effect to a track](/guides/effects/add-a-realtime-effect-to-a-track/).
