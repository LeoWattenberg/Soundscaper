## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

Bass and Treble is the tone control from a hi-fi amplifier: one knob for the low end, one for the high end, each in decibels. It is the fastest way to warm a thin voice or add air to a dull recording without learning a full equaliser.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → EQ and Filters → Bass and Treble**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick the recording that needs warming or brightening. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select all**.
4. Choose **Effect → EQ and filters → Bass and Treble**. In the **Bass and Treble** dialogue, set **Bass** to `4` and set **Treble** to `2`, then press **Apply to selection**. Small boosts go a long way. Four decibels of bass is clearly audible; more than about eight starts to boom.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The recording is fuller at the bottom and a little brighter on top.

## Tips

- Boosting adds level. If the waveform touches the top afterwards, lower **Volume** in the same dialogue or normalise.
- For precise control over particular frequencies, use **Filter Curve EQ** or **Graphic EQ** in the same submenu.
