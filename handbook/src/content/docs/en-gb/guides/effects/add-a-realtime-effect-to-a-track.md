The effects in the Effect menu alter the audio permanently. A realtime effect, however, sits on a track: it processes during playback and export, its settings are adjustable at any time, and the original audio remains untouched. This makes it ideal for reverb, EQ, and compression that you wish to continue adjusting as the mix evolves.

:::note[Transitioning from Audacity?]
This functionality aligns with Audacity's **Effect → Add Realtime Effects**. The names used below are specific to Soundscaper and may differ from Audacity's terminology.
:::

## Steps

1. Launch Soundscaper. A new, blank project is prepared upon loading the editor.
2. Select **File → Import** and choose the recording you want to apply the effect to. It will be placed as a clip on its own track.
3. Click **Effects** in the track header to open the Effects panel, then click **Effects** in the track rack and select **Reverb**. The settings window will open; click **Close** when you've finished adjusting it. The track rack will now display Reverb, and its settings window remains accessible from the rack for future adjustments.
4. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *Expected Observation:* The track plays through the applied reverb effect.

## Tips

- You can add multiple effects to a rack and rearrange them by dragging; each effect processes the output of the previous one.
- The master rack, located at the bottom of the Effects panel, processes the entire mix.
- The rack also includes Delay, high-pass and low-pass filters, Noise gate, Notch filter, Shelf filter, Tremolo, and Vocoder. These share a common set of core selections and rack controls.
- The [audio effects reference](/reference/generated/audio-effects/#regular-streaming-replacements) provides detailed explanations for Delay, Noise gate, and Vocoder behaviour, including the Vocoder’s normalization during playback.
