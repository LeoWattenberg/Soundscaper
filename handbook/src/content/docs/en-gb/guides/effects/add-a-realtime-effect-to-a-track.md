## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

The effects in the Effect menu permanently alter the audio. In contrast, a realtime effect sits on a track: it processes the audio during playback and export, its settings can be adjusted at any time, and the original audio remains untouched. This makes it ideal for reverb, EQ, and compression that you wish to continue fine-tuning as your mix evolves.

:::note[Transitioning from Audacity?]
This functionality corresponds to **Effect → Add Realtime Effects** in Audacity. The names used below are specific to Soundscaper and may differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Launch Soundscaper. A new, empty project is prepared upon loading the editor.
2. Select **File → Import** and choose the recording you want the effect to be applied to. It will be placed as a clip on its own track.
3. Click **Effects** in the track's header to open the Effects panel, then press **Effects** in the track's rack and select **Reverb**. The settings window will open; click **Close** when you have finished configuring it. The track’s rack will now display Reverb, and its settings window remains accessible from the rack whenever you need to adjust it.
4. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *Expected outcome:* The track plays through the applied reverb.

## Tips

- You can add multiple effects to a rack and drag them to reorder them; each effect processes the output of the previous one.
- The master rack, located at the bottom of the Effects panel, processes the entire mix.
- The rack also includes Delay, high-pass and low-pass filters, Noise gate, Notch filter, Shelf filter, Tremolo, and Vocoder. Their core selection and rack controls are shared among these effects.
- The [audio effects reference](/reference/generated/audio-effects/) details the behaviour of Delay, Noise gate, and Vocoder, including the Vocoder’s normalization during playback.
