## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

Slowing a passage down to learn it, or squeezing a voice-over into the time you have, used to mean the pitch moved with the speed. Change Tempo keeps the pitch where it is and only alters how fast the material goes by.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → Pitch and Tempo → Change Tempo**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import audio** and pick the recording to speed up or slow down. The file lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select all**.
4. Choose **Effect → Pitch and tempo → Change tempo**. In the **Change tempo** dialogue, set **Percent change** to `10`, then press **Apply to selection**. A positive value speeds the selection up; ten percent shortens it by roughly a tenth. Negative values slow it down.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The recording is a little faster and sounds the same in pitch.

## Tips

- Large changes — beyond about 30 percent — begin to sound processed. Apply a large change in two smaller steps if the result matters.
- The clip gets shorter or longer, so anything that follows it on the track moves.
