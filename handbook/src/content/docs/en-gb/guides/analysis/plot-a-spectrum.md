## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

A waveform shows how loud a sound is over time; a spectrum reveals its frequency composition. Plotting the spectrum of a problematic section is the quickest way to identify a 50 or 60 Hz hum, a monitor whistle, or the resonant frequency of a room – and then know where to adjust an equaliser.

:::note[From Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Analyse → Plot Spectrum**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which may differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Launch Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready upon loading the editor.
2. Select **File → Import** and choose the recording containing the issue. It will appear as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select All**.
4. Select **Analyse → Plot Spectrum**. The **Plot Spectrum** dialogue opens with the selection report.
   *You should see:* A graph of level against frequency for the selected section.

## Tips

- Isolate the problematic section only, such as a standalone hum, to prevent the spectrum from being overshadowed by surrounding music or speech.
- A tall, narrow spike indicates a tone; a broad plateau represents noise. Tones can be addressed with a narrow EQ cut, while noise requires [Noise Reduction](/guides/cleaning-up/remove-background-noise/).
