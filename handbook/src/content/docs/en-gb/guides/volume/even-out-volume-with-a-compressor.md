A compressor reduces the volume of loud moments once they exceed a set threshold, and then applies make-up gain to boost the overall result. Quiet words become more distinct, while shouts are prevented from overpowering the mix. This is why almost every spoken-word production utilises a compressor.

:::note[Transitioning from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → Volume and Compression → Compressor**. The names used below are specific to Soundscaper and may differ.
:::

## Instructions

1. Launch Soundscaper. A new, empty project will be ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Select **File → Import** and choose the recording with uneven levels. It will be imported as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select all**.
4. Navigate to **Effect → Volume and compression → Compressor**. In the **Compressor** dialog, set **Threshold** to `-18`, **Ratio** to `4`, and **Make-up gain** to `3`, then click **Apply to selection**. Any audio above −18 dB will be reduced at a ratio of 4:1; for every 4 decibels the input exceeds the threshold, the output increases by 1 decibel. The make-up gain compensates for any lost volume.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *Observation:* The loud and quiet sections should now have a more consistent volume level.

## Additional Advice

- A ratio of 2–4 works well for speech and sounds natural. Ratios of 10 and above function similarly to a limiter.
- If you notice the compressor 'pumping' audibly, consider increasing the **Release** time.
