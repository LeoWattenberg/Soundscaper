A limiter is a compressor with an extremely high ratio: it does nothing below the threshold, but anything above it is completely prevented from passing through. It's the ideal tool for taming stray peaks before exporting, or for boosting the overall level of a mix without clipping the peaks.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is the equivalent of Audacity's **Effect → Volume and Compression → Limiter**. The names used here are Soundscaper's own, which may differ slightly.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project will be ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and select the recording with the stray peaks. It will be added as a clip on its own track.
3. Select **Select → Select all**.
4. Go to **Effect → Volume and compression → Limiter**. In the **Limiter** dialog, set the **Threshold** to `-6` and then press **Apply to selection**. This will ensure nothing exceeds −6 dB. The make-up target then raises the result so the ceiling is close to full scale.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should notice:* The loudest moments are kept in check, while the rest of the audio remains unchanged.

## Tips

- Use a limiter for managing peaks and a [compressor](/guides/volume/even-out-volume-with-a-compressor/) for overall level balancing; they serve different purposes.
- Heavy limiting can make audio sound loud and flat. If you need to boost by more than a few decibels, it's likely your mix is too hot to begin with.
