## What this guide is for {#what-this-guide-is-for}

RMS normalisation adjusts a selection toward a target average signal level rather than a peak ceiling. It is useful when you need a consistent level across recordings; check the peaks afterward if the source is very dynamic.

:::note[Coming from Audacity?]
This is Audacity's **Effect → Volume and Compression → Loudness Normalization**. The names below are Soundscaper's own, which sometimes differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Open Soundscaper. A new, empty project is ready as soon as the editor loads.
2. Choose **File → Import** and pick the recording whose average level you want to set. It lands as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → All**.
4. Choose **Effect → Volume and compression → Loudness Normalization**. In the **Loudness Normalization** dialog, choose **RMS** for **Normalize**, set **Target RMS** to `-20` and turn off **Normalize stereo channels independently**, then press **Apply to selection**. A linked stereo adjustment preserves the balance between left and right while targeting −20 dB RMS.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *You should see:* The recording has been adjusted toward the −20 dB RMS target, with stereo balance preserved.

## Tips

- Use the same RMS target for recordings you want to compare at a similar average level.
- RMS normalisation does not guarantee a peak ceiling; check for clipping and leave headroom when needed.
