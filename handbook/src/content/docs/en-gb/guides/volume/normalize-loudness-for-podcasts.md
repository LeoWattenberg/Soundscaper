Podcast and streaming platforms measure loudness in LUFS, which reflects how loud a programme feels rather than the height of its peaks. Loudness Normalisation measures the entire selection and adjusts it to a target level, ensuring every episode you publish has a consistent volume.

:::note[Audacity users]
This feature is equivalent to Audacity's **Effect → Volume and Compression → Loudness Normalization**. The names used in Soundscaper may differ.
:::

## Steps

1. Launch Soundscaper. A new, empty project will open automatically.
2. Select **File → Import** and choose the quiet recording. It will be added as a clip on its own track.
3. Choose **Select → Select All**.
4. Go to **Effect → Volume and Compression → Loudness Normalization**. In the dialog box, set the **Target loudness** to `-16` and click **Apply to Selection**. For stereo podcasts, −16 LUFS is the standard target; for mono, −19 LUFS is often used. Refer to your platform's guidelines.
5. Press **Play** to listen, then **Stop**.
   *Expected result:* The recording should play at a consistent and comfortable volume.

## Tips

- Loudness Normalization can cause peaks to exceed full scale in highly dynamic material. Consider using a limiter or peak normalization to −1 dB if the waveform reaches the top.
- Apply this effect to the final mix, after all edits and other effects, to ensure the measurement accurately represents what listeners will hear.
